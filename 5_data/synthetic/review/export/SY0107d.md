## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting and confirmed that the conceptual design minutes had been sent.
- The industrial designer presented a clay model of the countertop stand, with a front tilted back by about twenty degrees, the two AA batteries in a weighted base, the two microphones behind slots at the front corners and a large stop button on the wood-effect top. He also presented the full cost breakdown, which totals 10.40 euros per unit, within the 12.50 euro limit.
- The team rejected a splash cover because it would block the microphones and agreed to keep the remaining budget as a buffer.
- The user interface designer presented the final display, with three colour-coded countdown rows, each beside its LED, and the final fixed voice command list. A user-chosen wake word was rejected on cost grounds.
- The marketing expert proposed the wake word 'Okay Timer', which the team adopted. She then led an evaluation against five criteria, and the design scored an average of about 1.8 on a scale from one to seven, where one is best. The weakest score was for noise handling, because of the microphone's two-metre range.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will write box text telling users to place the timer near where they cook.
- The user interface designer will make a quick start card with the voice command list to go in the box.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and the final report for management.
- The industrial designer will send the project manager the corrected cost sheet showing 10.40 euros.

**decisions**
- The production cost per unit is 10.40 euros, within the 12.50 euro limit.
- The remaining budget of about 2.10 euros will be kept as a buffer and not spent on extra features.
- No splash cover will be added, because it would cover the microphone slots and the casing is wipeable anyway.
- The stop button is placed on the wood-effect top of the stand, large enough to press with an elbow.
- The display has three rows (red, blue, green from top to bottom), each with a countdown and a matching LED to its left. When a timer finishes, its row flashes, its LED blinks and the timer is announced by colour.
- The voice command list is final and fixed: wake word, colour, time. The first free timer is used if no colour is given. Users can also say stop, cancel, ask how long is left and add time.
- The wake word will be 'Okay Timer'.
- The team was satisfied with the design after the evaluation, which averaged about 1.8.

**problems**
- The microphone only works reliably within about two metres, which gave the noise handling criterion the weakest score (three). Users will need to be told to place the timer near the hob.
- The cost of 10.40 euros is higher than the earlier rough estimate of 9.50 to 10 euros, mainly because of the wood-effect casing.
