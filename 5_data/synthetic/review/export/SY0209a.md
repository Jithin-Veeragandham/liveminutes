## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, the team introduced themselves, and she presented the project: a case for the company's existing Learnly seven-inch children's educational tablet.
- She gave the financial targets of a selling price of 25 Euros and a maximum production cost of 12.50 Euros per case, excluding packaging.
- The team agreed to keep the target age group of four to eight, and confirmed that the case only needs to fit the Learnly seven-inch tablet.
- In brainstorming, the industrial designer stressed drop protection using rubber or foam with thick corners. The team discussed a carrying handle, a handle that folds into a stand, bright colours that still appeal to parents, swappable covers, a screen flap versus a raised lip, easy cleaning, and a name tag window.
- The project manager then assigned the preparation tasks for the functional design meeting.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will research what parents and teachers want from the case, possibly with a short survey.
- The user interface designer will look at the functional side of the case, including the handle and access to the buttons and charging port.
- The industrial designer will research drop test standards and impact ratings. The user interface designer offered to do this first, but the industrial designer took it.
- The industrial designer will look into materials (rubber, foam, plastics) that fit the budget.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and send them round.
- Someone should check the retail prices of existing children's tablet cases. Nobody took this task.

**decisions**
- The target group is children aged four to eight.
- The case only has to fit the company's Learnly seven-inch tablet; there is no universal fit.
- The case should be made of an easy-to-clean, wipeable material.

**problems**
- It is unclear whether the 25 Euro price is competitive, because no one knows what competing cases cost, and no one was assigned to find out.
- The 12.50 Euro cost limit may rule out features that add parts, such as swappable covers.
- Whether the handle should double as a stand is open. The user interface designer worried that a foldable stand could pinch children's fingers.
- The name tag window idea was parked without a decision.
- The wide four-to-eight age range covers users with quite different needs.
