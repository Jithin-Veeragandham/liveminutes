## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting and welcomed back the marketing expert, who had missed the previous meeting. He reported that the minutes had been sent, that the Velodock spec sheet had been forwarded to the industrial designer, and that the dock screen can display a static QR code, so the QR fallback for unlocking is possible.
- The marketing expert presented the finished rider survey of sixty-three responses. Riders' top needs were seeing dock availability, unlocking quickly and knowing what a ride will cost. About a third of riders over sixty still want to use the old cards.
- The user interface designer presented rough mockups of the find, unlock and pay screens, and the team agreed on colour-coded map pins, showing the price before unlocking, and a ride screen with a timer and running cost.
- The industrial designer reported that the dock's NFC reader works with any NFC phone, but iPhones need the app open and a start-scan step, and the tap range is only two to three centimetres.
- The task of checking the payment provider's fees, offered by the industrial designer, was taken by the project manager. The project manager also took over asking the city transport office about the old cards from the marketing expert.

**actions**
- The project manager will ask the city transport office on Thursday whether the old cards stay valid. This task was first given to the marketing expert.
- The project manager will check the payment provider's per-transaction fees. The industrial designer had offered to do this.
- The project manager will ask Velodock for a sample dock unit for testing.
- The industrial designer will test the NFC tap on the sample unit with several phones, especially older Android phones.
- The user interface designer will turn the mockups into detailed designs, including the ride screen and the iPhone start-scan button.
- The marketing expert will draft the price wording for the unlock screen and send it to the user interface designer.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.

**decisions**
- Unlocking will use NFC, with a QR code shown on the dock screen as the fallback, now confirmed as possible by Velodock.
- Dock pins on the map will be colour-coded by the number of free bikes: green for plenty, amber for one or two, red for empty.
- The price will be shown on the unlock screen before the rider unlocks a bike.
- During the ride, the app will show a ride screen with a timer and the running cost. The screen closes and shows the total when the dock confirms the return.
- The iPhone version of the unlock screen will have a start-scan button, because iPhones require the app to be open for NFC.

**problems**
- It is still unknown whether the old cards will keep working after launch, which matters to about a third of riders over sixty.
- The payment provider's per-transaction fees are still unknown, and they matter for short rides of around ten minutes.
- The NFC tap has not yet been tested with the actual reader. The short range of two to three centimetres means the tap point on the dock must be clearly marked.
- Whether to offer a larger text mode for older riders was left open until the old-card question is answered.
- The reward scheme for returning bikes to emptier docks remains parked for budget reasons.
