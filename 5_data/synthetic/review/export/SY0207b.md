## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting with the full team present, confirmed that the user interface designer had received the brief by email, and recapped the kickoff decisions on multiple named timers and voice-first control with a physical stop button.
- The industrial designer presented the working design: a microphone, a small speaker, a voice recognition chip, a button and battery power, and she noted that an always-listening microphone with a wake word could drain AA batteries within a couple of months and that the chip's price is still unknown.
- The marketing expert reported a survey of about forty people: users dislike touching timers with dirty hands, often run several dishes at once and want three or four timers, need a loud alarm, and rarely want an app; voice competitors are expensive smart speakers, while simple timers sell for 5 to 15 euros.
- The user interface designer presented a small fixed command set with a wake word, short spoken confirmations, a fixed list of timer names plus numbered timers, and alarms that announce the timer's name.
- The team agreed on battery power, up to four named timers, the fixed command set, named spoken alarms, and dropping the phone app; a screen and the choice of languages remain open.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will check the cost of voice recognition chips; the marketing expert first offered to do this, but the industrial designer took it because she is already pricing the components.
- The industrial designer will estimate battery life with the always-listening wake word.
- The user interface designer will write out the full voice command list and all spoken responses.
- The marketing expert will find out which markets matter most and which languages to prioritise.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.
- The user interface designer was first asked to research whether competing timers offer app connectivity, but this task was dropped once the team decided against an app.

**decisions**
- The timer will be battery powered.
- The timer will support up to four named timers.
- Voice control will use a small fixed command set with a fixed list of timer names plus numbered timers.
- When a timer finishes, the alarm will loudly announce the timer's name.
- The phone app idea is dropped for good.

**problems**
- The cost of the voice recognition chip is still unknown.
- Battery life with an always-listening microphone may be short.
- The marketing expert proposed a small screen to show the timers; the industrial designer and user interface designer raised cost, battery and hands-free concerns, and the question was left open.
- It is not yet decided which languages to support at launch, and each language needs its own word list and voice recordings.
- The speaker must be loud enough to be heard over an extractor fan.
