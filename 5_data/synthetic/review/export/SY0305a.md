## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, noted that the industrial designer was absent because of a supplier visit, and had the marketing expert and user interface designer introduce themselves.
- The project manager presented the brief: a stand-alone self-checkout kiosk for medium-sized public libraries, to be original, trendy and user-friendly, sold at 3,500 euros per unit with a maximum production cost of 1,400 euros and a target of about 4,000 units in two years.
- The team discussed first ideas: the marketing expert stressed desk queues and the need to process a stack of books at once, which the user interface designer linked to RFID, although some libraries still use barcodes.
- The marketing expert raised accessibility for older users, children and wheelchair users, and the idea of matching library branding, possibly with a removable printable front panel.
- The user interface designer declared the kiosk would be touchscreen only, but this was questioned and the project manager deferred it to the next meeting.
- The project manager closed by assigning individual tasks for the functional design meeting, including the working design for the absent industrial designer.

**actions**
- The industrial designer (absent) is to work on the working design: hardware components such as barcode scanner versus RFID pad, screen and casing, costed against the 1,400 euro limit.
- The project manager will email the industrial designer the notes and her assignment.
- The user interface designer will do the technical functions design, sketching the borrow and return flows.
- The marketing expert will research user requirements from library users and staff, including talking to the Wilson Street branch, and roughly check competitor pricing.
- The project manager will send out the minutes.

**decisions**
- The kiosk must be able to process a stack of books at once rather than one book at a time.
- Accessibility (kiosk height, text size, suitability for older users, children and wheelchair users) will be treated as a requirement.

**problems**
- Whether to support RFID only or also include a barcode scanner is unresolved; RFID-only would exclude libraries still using barcodes, while adding a scanner raises cost and complexity.
- The user interface designer's claim that the kiosk will be touchscreen only with no physical buttons was not agreed and was deferred to the next meeting.
- The 1,400 euro production cost limit, including screen, scanner and casing, is considered tight.
