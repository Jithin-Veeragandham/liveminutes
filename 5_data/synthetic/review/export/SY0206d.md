## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting and noted that the user interface designer was absent but had put her detailed designs in the shared folder.
- He reported that the old cards stay valid for at least the first year after launch, so the team decided against a separate large text mode and chose to have the app follow the phone's system text size.
- He also reported that the payment provider charges twenty cents per transaction plus 1.5 percent, so the team decided to charge each ride once at the end, with a pre-authorisation hold at unlock.
- The marketing expert presented his price wording, which riders from the survey understood easily, and the industrial designer reported NFC test results on the Velodock sample unit, which led to 'hold' wording, a QR prompt after five seconds and a tap-point decal on the docks.
- The team reviewed the user interface designer's screens and signed off the design pending her changes, while the reward scheme raised again by the marketing expert stayed parked for budget reasons.

**actions**
- The user interface designer will update the unlock screen with the price wording, the 'hold' wording, the QR prompt after five seconds and the decal icon.
- The user interface designer will check her screens at the largest system text size, especially the ride screen.
- The project manager will give the user interface designer the list of changes when she is back on Monday.
- The industrial designer will finalise the decal artwork this week.
- The industrial designer will send the decal icon file to the user interface designer.
- The project manager will send the final design pack to management once the screens are updated.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.
- The marketing expert will write a short launch note for older riders explaining that the old cards still work.
- Checking the cost of printing and fitting weatherproof decals on all docks was mentioned, but nobody took this task.

**decisions**
- There will be no separate large text mode; the app will follow the phone's system text size.
- Each ride will be charged as one transaction at the end, when the dock confirms the return, with a pre-authorisation hold at unlock.
- The unlock screen will use the price wording 'One euro to unlock, then ten cents a minute. You pay when you return the bike.'
- The unlock screen will tell riders to hold their phone on the tap point, and will prompt them to use the QR code if nothing happens after five seconds.
- The tap point on each dock will be marked with a round decal with a phone icon, and the same icon will be used in the app.
- The detailed design is signed off, pending the user interface designer's changes.

**problems**
- The cost of printing and fitting weatherproof decals on all docks is unknown, and nobody took the task of checking it.
- The budget stands at about 74,000 of the 80,000 euros with the decal cost still unknown.
- The spring launch timeline is still tight.
- Older Android phones need to be held on the reader for about two seconds, and phones without NFC rely on the QR fallback.
- The old cards are only confirmed valid for the first year after launch, after which the transport office will review them.
- The reward scheme for returning bikes to emptier docks remains parked for budget reasons.
