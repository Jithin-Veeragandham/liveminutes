## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, noted that the marketing expert was back from the trade fair in Hamburg, and recapped the kickoff decisions.
- The industrial designer reported on materials: bamboo fibre was ruled out because its melamine binder can migrate into hot drinks and it cracks in commercial dishwashers, while polypropylene is food-safe, heat-resistant, rated for around five hundred commercial wash cycles and quoted at roughly 1.60 euros per cup and lid, so the team chose polypropylene.
- The user interface designer presented the over-the-counter return flow, and the team agreed that the deposit covers cup and lid as a set, leaving the case of a cup returned without its lid open.
- The marketing expert presented competitor schemes, where one-euro deposits led to low return rates, and the team adopted her recommendation of a two-euro deposit.
- After the marketing expert reported that café chains need more than one size, the team revised its earlier confirmation of a single size and settled on two sizes, 250 ml and 350 ml, sharing one lid.
- Revisiting the app idea from the kickoff, the team agreed on a laser-etched code on the cup base that cafés scan on return, with no customer app for now.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will get a supplier quote for the two cup sizes with the shared lid, including the laser-etched code.
- The user interface designer will update the return flow with the café scanning step and what the barista needs at the counter.
- The user interface designer will add options for handling cups returned without a lid to the return flow.
- The marketing expert will check with the café chains that 250 ml and 350 ml fit their menus, and ask them about cups returned without lids.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and send them to the team.

**decisions**
- The cup will be made of polypropylene; bamboo fibre is ruled out.
- The deposit will be two euros.
- The deposit covers the cup and lid together as a set.
- The cup will come in two sizes, 250 ml and 350 ml, with one shared lid and the same deposit for both; this replaced the single-size plan confirmed earlier in the meeting.
- Cups will carry a laser-etched code on the base that cafés scan on return; there will be no customer app for now.

**problems**
- How to handle a cup returned without its lid is unresolved; a partial refund was considered too fiddly for baristas.
- What scanning equipment cafés need at the counter is not yet worked out.
- The cost impact of a second cup mould is only estimated at ten to twenty cents per unit and needs a proper quote.
- Whether 250 ml and 350 ml fit the café chains' menus still needs confirmation.
