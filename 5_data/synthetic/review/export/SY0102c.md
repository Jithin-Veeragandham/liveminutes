## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting and reported that the corporate colours were now in the shared folder and that the client accepts losing about eighty cents per unreturned cup, so the one-euro deposit stays; the client will count stock per shop and does not need individual cup tracking, so the team dropped the QR code on the bottom of the cup.
- The industrial designer showed a standard polypropylene sample from the Rotterdam plant, which the team found light and somewhat cheap-looking, and reported a matte finish at about twelve cents extra and two-colour logo printing at about ten cents; the team chose a matte finish.
- The marketing expert presented competitor research showing muted, earthy colours and slightly tapered shapes that stack well, and the team agreed on a dark green matte, slightly tapered cup with a white logo in one size.
- The user interface designer presented the cup markings (white logo on one side, a return message on the other, an embossed one-euro symbol on the base) and a three-step counter sign, which were accepted.
- On the lid, the industrial designer recommended a sip lid with a slider, rated for the full five hundred washes, and the team adopted it after first leaning towards a flip lid; this brings the cost to about two euros seven per cup, under the three-euro limit.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will ask the Rotterdam plant for a sample in the client's dark green, matte, with the slider sip lid, using the hex codes from the shared folder.
- The industrial designer will prepare a full cost breakdown of the final design.
- The user interface designer will produce the final artwork for the cup markings and the counter sign, trying the wording "any of our forty shops".
- The marketing expert will put together evaluation criteria for judging the final design at the detailed design meeting.
- The marketing expert will show the sample cup to a few students at a campus shop to get a quick reaction once the sample is available.
- The project manager will send the client the concept (dark green matte cup, logo and slider lid) for approval before the design is finalised.
- The project manager will write the minutes.

**decisions**
- The deposit stays at one euro; the client accepts the loss of about eighty cents on each unreturned cup.
- No QR code will be printed on the bottom of the cup, since the client will count stock per shop instead of tracking individual cups.
- The cup will have a matte finish.
- The cup will be dark green with a white logo and a slightly tapered shape that stacks, in a single size for now.
- The cup markings are: the white logo on one side about halfway up, the line "Bring me back to any counter, get your euro back" on the other side, and an embossed one-euro symbol on the base.
- The lid will be a sip lid with a clip-in slider, at about two euros seven per cup in total; this replaces the flip lid the team had first chosen in the meeting.

**problems**
- The plant may not be able to match the client's exact dark green.
- The slider sip lid does not close as tightly as a flip lid, although it does not spill when tipped slightly.
- Offering two cup sizes was raised and parked; the team stays with one size for now.
- The client has not yet approved the concept.
- The counter sign wording ("any shop" or "any of our forty shops") is still to be tried out.
