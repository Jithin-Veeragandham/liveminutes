## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting and noted that the marketing expert was absent through illness, so no user requirements or survey results could be presented; the team agreed to continue on the technical side and flag anything needing marketing input.
- The industrial designer reported component costs from his supplier visit: an offline voice recognition chip at 2.50 euros handling about twenty phrases plus one wake word, a noise-cancelling microphone at 0.80, a speaker at 1.20 and an LCD at 1.50, totalling 6 euros before batteries, board and casing, with an estimated final cost of 8 to 9 euros.
- He explained that the chip's low-power wake-word mode allows about three months on three AA batteries, and the team decided on battery power and a wake word, leaving the exact wake word open.
- The user interface designer presented a draft command list (set, cancel, how long left, stop, add a minute); because each number uses a phrase slot, the team agreed to have numbers spoken as digit sequences, and settled the parked multiple-timer question by allowing up to two numbered timers.
- The team agreed on a beep plus display for feedback rather than spoken replies, confirmed a single physical button, assigned actions, and scheduled the conceptual design meeting for Wednesday at 11:00.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will cost the casing.
- The industrial designer will get a sample speaker from the supplier and test its loudness next to an extractor fan.
- The user interface designer will update the command list for two timers and digit-based numbers.
- The user interface designer will sketch the display layout.
- The project manager will send the minutes to the marketing expert, including the decisions on the button and battery power.
- The marketing expert is to email the survey results.
- The marketing expert is to try out wake word options with the survey participants.

**decisions**
- The timer will use a wake word; the exact word is left open pending marketing input.
- The timer will be battery powered, using three AA batteries.
- Numbers will be spoken as digit sequences so the command set fits the chip's roughly twenty phrases.
- The timer will support at most two timers, numbered timer one and timer two, without custom names.
- Feedback will be a beep plus the display; spoken replies are on hold for cost reasons.
- There will be one physical button: press to stop the alarm, hold to cancel.
- The conceptual design meeting will be held on Wednesday at 11:00.

**problems**
- The casing has not been costed, so the margin under the 10-euro production cost is uncertain, with the estimate at 8 to 9 euros.
- Speaker loudness over an extractor fan has not been tested.
- The exact wake word has not been chosen.
- User requirements and survey results were not available because the marketing expert was absent.
- Spoken replies would cost about 0.40 to 0.50 euros extra and were postponed rather than ruled out.
