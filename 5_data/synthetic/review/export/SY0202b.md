## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, noted that the user interface designer was absent through illness, and confirmed that he had sent round the brief and the kickoff notes. He recapped the earlier decisions: checkout and renewals only, and one scanner for library cards and app QR codes.
- The industrial designer presented the working design: an embedded PC, a fifteen-inch touchscreen, a card and QR scanner, a barcode or RFID book reader, and a small thermal printer. Without RFID, his rough cost estimate came to about 2,900 euros, and the wood-panelled casing was the largest cost.
- The marketing expert presented the user requirements and the accessibility requirements from library services. These covered speed, the fact that about a third of borrowers want printed slips, wheelchair reach heights, audio output, and a physical button.
- The team agreed on email receipts by default with optional printing, and on one hardware help button with everything else on the touchscreen. They also agreed to lower the book shelf to accessible heights.
- The RFID pricing task was first given to the industrial designer and then passed to the marketing expert, because library services already has an RFID supplier price list. The barcode versus RFID decision was left open until those numbers are available.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will get RFID reader and tag prices from library services' existing supplier. This task was first assigned to the industrial designer.
- The user interface designer, who was absent, will update the checkout and renewal flow for the help button, audio mode and print option, and will send round her draft screens before the next meeting.
- The marketing expert will email the accessibility requirements to the user interface designer today.
- The industrial designer will prepare sketches of the casing look and materials, using the new accessible heights.
- The project manager will write up and circulate the notes and brief the user interface designer on the button decision.

**decisions**
- Receipts will be sent by email by default, and borrowers can choose to print one; the printer stays in the design.
- The kiosk will have one physical help button. The button calls staff, and holding it switches on audio mode. All other controls stay on the touchscreen.
- The layout will follow the accessibility heights: the book shelf will be about 75 to 80 cm instead of 90 cm, and no controls will be above about 120 cm.
- The kiosk will provide audio support, with a headphone jack and spoken instructions, for visually impaired borrowers.

**problems**
- The choice between barcode and RFID, or supporting both, is still open until the RFID prices are known.
- It is unclear whether adding RFID keeps the unit under the 4,000-euro production cost target.
- Some library branches may still be barcode-only and not RFID-tagged.
- The user interface designer has not yet presented the flow or screens, and she was absent when the hardware button was decided against her earlier all-touchscreen proposal.
