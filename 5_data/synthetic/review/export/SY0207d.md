## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting with the full team present, welcomed back the user interface designer after her illness, confirmed that she had received his email and that the previous minutes had been sent, and recapped the conceptual design decisions.
- The industrial designer presented a palm-sized clay mock-up of the puck, about nine centimetres across, with a large central stop button, side speaker grille, four-LED ring, magnet and sealed battery door, and gave a detailed cost breakdown of 11.45 euros per unit for a white-only case, still excluding voice recording costs.
- The user interface designer reported that she had translated the command list into German and French, adjusting some timer names, and that the wake word 'Hey timer' was poorly recognised from French speakers; the team adopted the made-up wake word 'Tilly' for all three languages.
- The marketing expert checked the concept against the survey and found that users wanted to lower the alarm volume, so the team added 'louder' and 'quieter' voice commands at no extra cost; his renewed proposal for pastel colours was rejected in favour of launching in white only.
- The task of getting a native-speaker recording quote was given to the marketing expert, and the speaker loudness test moved from the industrial designer to the user interface designer; in a brief evaluation the team judged the product good, with alarm loudness over an extractor fan as the main remaining risk.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will get a quote from the survey agency for native-speaker recordings of the spoken responses in English, German and French; the user interface designer had first offered to look into recording studios.
- The user interface designer will test the speaker's loudness against a cooker hood using a sample speaker sent by the industrial designer; this task was first given to the industrial designer.
- The user interface designer will add the 'louder' and 'quieter' commands to the command list in all three languages.
- The user interface designer will put the final translated command list in the shared folder.
- The industrial designer will update the cost breakdown with the recording quote and send round the final figure.
- The project manager will send round the minutes and write the final report for management.

**decisions**
- The wake word will be 'Tilly' in all three languages.
- Volume will be adjusted by the voice commands 'louder' and 'quieter', with no extra button or dial.
- The timer will launch in white only.

**problems**
- The cost of native-speaker recordings is still unknown, and the 11.45 euro estimate leaves about one euro of margin for it.
- The speaker has not yet been tested for loudness over an extractor fan.
- The marketing expert still wants coloured versions; colours may be revisited after launch but nothing was decided.
- The marketing expert's idea of using 'Tilly' as the product name was not decided.
