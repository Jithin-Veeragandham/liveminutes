## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting with all four members present and confirmed that the marketing expert had received her email about his research task and the minutes.
- The industrial designer reported that the firm supplier quotes came to about 1,240 euros, up from the earlier estimate of 1,185 euros but still about 160 euros under the 1,400 euro limit, with the printed front panel at about 45 euros.
- The marketing expert reported that roughly a third of medium-sized libraries still use barcodes and that nearly all require a PIN, which confirmed the barcode scanner and keypad; he also relayed that returns cause the biggest desk queues at Wilson Street.
- The user interface designer presented the borrow and return mockups with a three-button start screen, a large text mode, and an account screen that shows fines but directs users to pay at the desk. He also declared a built-in return slot with a drop bin, but this was not agreed and was set aside.
- The team chose a powder-coated steel body in warm white, carrying the branding on the printed front panel, with mains power, a standard PC board and a screen at about 100 cm tilted back. The project manager then assigned tasks for the detailed design meeting.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will build the prototype model of the kiosk.
- The user interface designer will give the industrial designer the screen layout for the prototype and will finish the screens in the branded version, including the large text mode.
- The marketing expert will prepare evaluation criteria for the detailed design meeting.
- The marketing expert will check with the Wilson Street branch what they would want on a front panel.
- The industrial designer will update the cost sheet with the steel body and warm white finish.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.

**decisions**
- The barcode scanner and PIN keypad stay in the design, because about a third of libraries still use barcodes and nearly all require PINs.
- The kiosk body will be powder-coated sheet steel with the removable printed front panel carrying the branding and colour.
- The body colour will be warm white.
- The kiosk will run on mains power with a standard small PC board.
- The screen will be at about 100 cm, tilted back, so it is usable standing and from a wheelchair.
- As a provisional layout to be checked on the prototype, the RFID pad will sit on a shelf to the right of the screen, with the scanner and keypad on the left.
- The screen colours will follow the library's branding but will always be high contrast, with dark text on light.

**problems**
- How returned books are handled is still unresolved. The proposal for a built-in return slot and drop bin was not agreed, since it would double the kiosk size, the bin needs emptying and capacity is unknown.
- The cost margin is only about 160 euros under the 1,400 euro limit.
- The left and right placement of the keypad, scanner and RFID pad still needs checking on the prototype.
- Children under about eight may not reach the screen comfortably and will need a parent's help.
- Barcode-only libraries will still have to scan books one at a time.
