## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting and confirmed he had sent out the kickoff minutes, the brief and the shared folder link.
- The industrial designer reported back from the dock supplier: the docks use Bluetooth Low Energy with a QR sticker as backup, there is no NFC, and there is no screen, only an LED ring. The team chose Bluetooth as the main unlock method with QR as the fallback.
- The user interface designer presented rough wireframes for the find, unlock and return flows. The marketing expert presented the rider survey results: the top request was seeing free return slots, the second was knowing the price before a ride, and only about eighteen percent were interested in a leaderboard. The leaderboard was dropped for the pilot.
- The marketing expert's competitor review showed that most one-star reviews concern failed unlocks and returns that were never registered. This led the team to add a proper return confirmation screen showing the cost.
- The project manager summarised the functions: find with bikes and free slots on the map, one-tap Bluetooth unlock with QR fallback and the price shown beforehand, and return with confirmation. Payment stays at sign-up, and a high-contrast mode will be added for night use. The next meeting was set for Tuesday at two.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will ask the dock supplier how long it takes for a dock to report a return.
- The user interface designer will update the wireframes. The updates are: free slots on the map, a return confirmation with the cost, the price before unlock, a QR fallback screen, and a prompt to switch on Bluetooth.
- The user interface designer will check guidance on outdoor readability, such as contrast and text size. This task was first given to the marketing expert.
- The marketing expert will upload the survey results and the competitor review notes to the shared folder.
- The project manager will send out the minutes and an invite for the next meeting.

**decisions**
- Bluetooth will be the main way to unlock a bike, with the dock's QR sticker as the fallback.
- The leaderboard is dropped for the pilot, given weak survey interest.
- The map will show free return slots as well as available bikes.
- The return flow will end with a confirmation screen stating that the bike is locked, the ride has ended and what was paid.
- The price will be shown to the rider before unlocking.
- Payment setup stays at sign-up, as agreed at the kickoff.
- The app will have a high-contrast mode for night use.
- If Bluetooth is off on the phone, the app will prompt the rider to switch it on.
- The next meeting (conceptual design) will be on Tuesday at two.

**problems**
- It is not yet known how quickly a dock reports a return to the system. This matters for the return confirmation.
- The Bluetooth range is only about two metres, so riders must stand at the dock.
- Riders may not understand the dock's LED colours, so the phone has to carry all feedback.
- The QR fallback remains unreliable in rain and at night.
- The readability standards for outdoor and night use still need to be checked.
