## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting and welcomed back the user interface designer, who confirmed that he had received her email with the decisions and chip prices from the functional design meeting.
- The marketing expert presented the list of ten timer names taken from the survey: pasta, rice, eggs, potatoes, vegetables, roast, oven, bread, cake and tea. The user interface designer showed that the names, the wake word, four commands and the number words use exactly the forty words the chip can recognise, and the team kept all ten names.
- The industrial designer reported a speaker quote of 1.40 euros, which brings voice input and output to 4.20 euros, and a backup battery quote of about 0.90 euros against an estimated unit cost of about 11.10 euros. The team left the backup battery out for now.
- The user interface designer presented a backlit segment LCD with large digits and printed icons for the timer names. The team rejected his touch strip in favour of a rotary dial with a separate cancel button.
- For the casing, the team chose a splash-proof, wipe-clean ABS body with rubber seals and a flat display window, available in white and black.

**actions**
- The industrial designer and the user interface designer will build a mock-up model of the casing with the dial and the display for the detailed design meeting.
- The industrial designer will prepare a full cost breakdown, including whether the backup battery fits the budget.
- The user interface designer will write up the final forty-word vocabulary list with example phrases and decide the alarm wording.
- The marketing expert will put together evaluation criteria from the survey to check the design against.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.

**decisions**
- All ten timer names from the marketing expert's list are kept, which uses the chip's full forty-word vocabulary, so no words can be added later.
- The speaker costs 1.40 euros, so voice input and output together cost 4.20 euros.
- The backup battery is left out for now and may be added back if the final costing leaves room.
- The display will be a backlit segment LCD that shows one timer in large digits, with printed icons for the timer names and for other running timers.
- The button fallback will be a rotary dial (turn to set, push to start, push and hold to choose a timer) plus a separate cancel button. The touch strip was rejected.
- The casing will be an ABS plastic body with rubber seals around the dial and button and a flat display window. It will be splash-proof and wipe-clean, but not dishwasher safe.
- The timer will come in white and black.

**problems**
- The vocabulary is at the chip's limit of forty words, so there is no room to add any commands or names later.
- The estimated unit cost of about 11.10 euros leaves little margin, and whether the 0.90 euro backup battery can be included depends on the full cost breakdown.
- A third colour, such as sage green, is still open.
- The marketing expert's idea of a fridge magnet was not adopted because of the mains cable, and is only being kept in mind.
- The exact alarm wording (for example 'pasta is done') is not yet decided.
