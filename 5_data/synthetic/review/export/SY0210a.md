## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting for a voice-controlled kitchen timer and introduced the team, noting that the user interface designer was absent.
- She presented the project aim and budget: a selling price of 25 euros, a maximum production cost of 12.50 euros per unit including all components, and an overall profit target of 50 million euros.
- The team discussed initial requirements, including kitchen background noise as a challenge for voice recognition, the need for a manual backup control, the target market, and the open question of battery versus mains power.
- A planned new survey of home cooks was dropped after the marketing expert pointed out that last year's company kitchen appliance survey already covers timers and cooking habits; he will extract the relevant data instead.
- The user interface designer was given the voice command and spoken feedback work in her absence, and the next meeting will be the functional design meeting.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will extract the timer and cooking-habit findings from last year's kitchen appliance survey for the next meeting.
- The industrial designer will research microphones, speech recognition chips and power options, with rough costs.
- The user interface designer (absent) will work out the voice command list and how the timer responds to the user.
- The user interface designer (absent) will also look at the idea of named, multiple simultaneous timers.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer with her tasks and the meeting notes, and will circulate the minutes.

**decisions**
- The timer will have at least one physical button as a backup to voice control.
- The target market is home cooks rather than professional kitchens.
- The new survey of home cooks will not be carried out; existing survey data from last year will be used instead.

**problems**
- Kitchen background noise may stop the timer from hearing commands, and using more than one microphone would add cost within a tight 12.50 euro budget.
- Whether the timer runs on batteries or plugs in is unresolved: always-on listening drains batteries, while a cable is unpopular on counters.
- The industrial designer's suggestion of a magnetic back for mounting on a fridge or cooker hood was not agreed and was deferred to the conceptual design meeting.
- The marketing expert's idea of named, multiple simultaneous timers was not decided and was passed to the user interface designer.
