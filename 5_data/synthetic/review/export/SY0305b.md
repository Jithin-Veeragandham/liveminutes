## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, noted that the marketing expert was absent because he was interviewing staff at the Wilson Street branch, and confirmed that the industrial designer had received her assignment by email.
- The industrial designer presented a rough component costing: RFID pad, touchscreen, PC board, receipt printer and a casing with a removable printable front panel came to about 1,100 euros, rising to about 1,185 euros once a barcode scanner and a PIN keypad were added, which is within the 1,400 euro limit.
- The team agreed to combine RFID stack processing with a small barcode scanner for libraries that have not switched to RFID.
- The user interface designer presented the borrow, return and account functions. A fine payment feature was proposed but rejected because a card payment terminal would push the cost over the limit.
- The touchscreen-only question deferred from the kickoff was settled. The project manager relayed the marketing expert's notes that older users struggle with touchscreens and that competitor kiosks sell for 4,000 to 6,000 euros, and the team agreed on a touchscreen with a physical keypad for PIN entry only.
- The project manager assigned the absent marketing expert a research task and closed the meeting with actions for next time.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will get firm supplier quotes for all components, including the barcode scanner and PIN keypad, and will check the front panel price with the printing supplier.
- The user interface designer will produce mockups of the borrow and return screens, including a large text mode.
- The marketing expert (absent) is to find out roughly what share of libraries still use barcodes and whether libraries require patrons to use a PIN. The project manager assigned this task.
- The project manager will email the marketing expert his task and will send out the minutes.

**decisions**
- The kiosk will use an RFID pad for stack processing plus a small barcode scanner for libraries still on barcodes.
- Fine payment will not be included on the kiosk in this version. Users can view fines but must pay at the desk.
- The kiosk will use a touchscreen for all interaction plus a physical keypad for PIN entry only.

**problems**
- Where returned books go after check-in is unresolved, because a return bin with a sorter would be a separate, expensive machine.
- Barcode-only libraries will have to scan books one at a time rather than processing a stack.
- The share of libraries still using barcodes and whether libraries use patron PINs are unknown until the marketing expert reports back.
- The cost figures are rough estimates pending firm supplier quotes, including the front panel price.
