## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting, noted that the user interface designer was absent through illness but had sent her full command list, confirmed the previous minutes had been sent, and recapped the functional design decisions.
- The industrial designer reported that a full speech recognition chip costs about 7 euros while a fixed vocabulary chip costs about 2.80 euros, and that an always-listening microphone would drain two AA batteries in five or six weeks, but a low-power wake-word circuit would extend this to about four months for around 30 cents more.
- She proposed a palm-sized, splash-proof plastic puck with a magnet on the back, a central stop button and a side-facing speaker, and suggested a ring of four LEDs instead of a screen; the marketing expert accepted this after first arguing again for a small display.
- The marketing expert identified the UK, Germany and France as the key markets and the team agreed to launch in English, German and French; his proposal to offer three pastel colours was left open pending cost.
- The industrial designer's rough cost estimate came to about 11.30 euros per unit, under the 12.50 euro limit but before any voice recording costs.

**actions**
- The user interface designer, who was absent, will adapt the command list and spoken responses for German and French and check that the wake word works in all three languages; the project manager will email her the details.
- The industrial designer will prepare a detailed cost breakdown and a clay mock-up of the case for the next meeting.
- The marketing expert will check the concept against the survey requirements.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.
- Checking the cost of having the voice responses recorded by native speakers was mentioned, but nobody took it on.

**decisions**
- The timer will use a fixed vocabulary voice recognition chip.
- The timer will use a low-power wake-word circuit and run on two AA batteries.
- The case will be a round, splash-proof plastic puck with a magnet on the back, a central stop button and a side-facing speaker.
- There will be no screen; a ring of four LEDs, one per timer, will show running and finished timers.
- The timer will launch in English, German and French.

**problems**
- The cost of recording the spoken responses with native speakers in three languages is unknown and no one is responsible for checking it.
- The marketing expert's proposal to offer the timer in three colours was not decided because of extra tooling costs.
- The estimated cost of 11.30 euros leaves little margin for colours or recording costs.
- The wake word may not work well in all three languages and needs checking.
