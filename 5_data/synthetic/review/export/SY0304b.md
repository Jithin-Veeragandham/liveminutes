## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting with all four members present. She welcomed the industrial designer, who had missed the kickoff, and confirmed she had emailed him the brief and minutes.
- The marketing expert reported survey results from twenty-four people (fourteen students, eight staff, two visitors). Users want to read the number from the road before turning in, liked the colour coding, did not want adverts, and often asked for an app, which remains a later idea.
- The industrial designer compared barrier counters (about seventy pounds per entrance, but prone to drift from tailgating) with per-bay sensors (about thirty pounds per bay, over five thousand pounds for the library car park). He also recommended an IP65 outdoor casing and an LED number panel instead of an LCD screen.
- The user interface designer presented three layouts in two sizes. The team chose the larger sixty-centimetre LED display with the car park name on top and agreed the colour thresholds. They settled power on mains from the barrier supply, because the underground library car parks get no sun, and chose barrier counters with a nightly reset. This made the per-bay sensor quote the project manager had requested earlier in the meeting unnecessary, so it was cancelled.
- The estimated cost per sign is around four hundred pounds. The central sign near the main gate is still undecided pending input from the estates office.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will check the mains connection at each barrier with the estates office and specify the casing properly.
- The user interface designer will produce the final layout at sixty centimetres, LED, with the car park name on top and the agreed colour thresholds.
- The marketing expert will survey more visitors at the main gate during next week's open day.
- The project manager will ask the estates office whether they want the central sign near the main gate and whether there is separate budget for it.
- The project manager will send the minutes round.

**decisions**
- The display will be the larger size, about sixty centimetres wide.
- The number will be shown on an LED number panel rather than an LCD screen.
- The car park name will appear on top of the number.
- Colour thresholds: green above twenty free spaces, amber at twenty or fewer, red at five or fewer.
- The signs will run on mains power taken from the existing barrier supply, not solar.
- Sensing will use barrier counters with a nightly reset, and the attendant can enter a correction.

**problems**
- Making the number flash below ten free spaces was stated as settled by the user interface designer, but nobody agreed and the point was left unresolved.
- The estimated cost of about four hundred pounds per sign leaves no margin in the budget.
- Barrier counters can still drift during the day because of tailgating, which the nightly reset only partly addresses.
- It is undecided whether the central sign near the main gate will be included. It would need data from every car park via cabling or a wireless link.
- Visitors are under-represented in the survey, with only two respondents.
- Many users asked for an app, which remains out of scope for now.
