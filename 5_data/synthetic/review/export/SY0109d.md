## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting, welcomed back the user interface designer and confirmed that he had sent the minutes and the invite and had briefed her on the decisions of the previous meeting.
- The industrial designer reported that the dock supplier confirmed the dock sends its lock status, with a lock timestamp, to the phone over Bluetooth. This means the two-stage return confirmation works and billing can stop at the lock time. The supplier can also fit external antennas at the two underground car park docks, which will take three weeks.
- The marketing expert presented the outdoor readability check: a contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and buttons, and body text of at least sixteen point. She reported that the council's final brand guidelines use a slightly darker teal and require the logo at the top left of the header. The user interface designer confirmed the darker teal passes contrast with white text.
- The user interface designer presented the updated wireframes with the 'bike locked, confirming' state and the receipt notification, and the team added a short vibration when the bike locks.
- The team first chose yellow for the night mode main button, but later changed this to the bright teal variant from the council guidelines, because yellow is the council's warning colour. A mock-up review was set for Friday week at ten.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will get a confirmed fitting date in writing from the dock supplier for the antennas at the underground car park docks.
- The user interface designer will produce high-fidelity mock-ups including all final decisions by the end of next week.
- The industrial designer will write a short spec of the Bluetooth lock message and timestamp for the developers.
- The marketing expert will upload the readability findings and the final brand guidelines to the shared folder.
- The marketing expert will draft a launch message to the pilot waiting list explaining how unlocking works, including the QR fallback.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and the final report for the council.

**decisions**
- The two-stage return confirmation stays, now that the dock is confirmed to send its lock status with a timestamp over Bluetooth, and billing stops at the dock's lock timestamp.
- The darker teal from the council's final brand guidelines is used for buttons and headers, and the council logo sits at the top left of the header.
- The night mode main button will be the council's bright teal variant for dark backgrounds. This was revised during the meeting from an earlier choice of yellow.
- Body text will be at least sixteen point, with a contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text.
- The phone will give a short vibration when the bike locks.
- A free first ride code will not be designed into sign-up for the pilot.
- The team will review the user interface designer's mock-ups on Friday week at ten.

**problems**
- The antenna fitting at the underground car park docks takes three weeks and may be tight for the pilot start; no confirmed fitting date has been given yet.
- The bright teal night mode button passes the contrast check but with less margin than yellow.
- Riders may not know about the QR fallback, which needs to be explained in the launch communications.
- A free first ride offer would be a council pricing decision and was left outside the app design.
