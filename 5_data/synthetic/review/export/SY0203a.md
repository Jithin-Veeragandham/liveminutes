## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, introduced the team and presented the project: a protective case for a children's educational tablet that should be original, trendy and user-friendly.
- She gave the financial targets: a selling price of 30 euros and a maximum production cost of 15 euros per unit. The industrial designer noted that this is tight for a properly shockproof case.
- After an icebreaker drawing exercise, the team discussed first ideas: drop protection with rubber or foam and thick corners, a carrying handle, cutouts that keep the buttons and camera clear, a possible stand, and a wipeable material such as silicone or EVA foam.
- The team agreed to target children aged four to eight.
- The task of finding the tablet sizes the case must fit was first given to the industrial designer. It was then reassigned to the user interface designer, so the industrial designer could concentrate on materials.

**actions**
- The user interface designer will find the common tablet sizes and dimensions the case must fit. This task was reassigned from the industrial designer.
- The user interface designer will work out the basic functions the case needs to provide for the user.
- The marketing expert will research competitor cases and their prices.
- The industrial designer will research materials, such as foam versus silicone, and estimate what fits within the 15 euro production cost.
- The project manager will send round notes from the meeting.
- Checking safety regulations for children's products, such as small parts and choking hazards, was raised but nobody took it on.

**decisions**
- The target age group for the product is children aged four to eight.

**problems**
- It is unclear whether the case will fit one specific tablet or several models.
- The 15 euro production cost limit may make strong shock protection difficult.
- A stand was suggested but would add cost. It was not decided.
- A shoulder strap was suggested, with doubts about its suitability for four-year-olds. It was deferred to the next meeting.
- No one is responsible for checking child safety regulations.
