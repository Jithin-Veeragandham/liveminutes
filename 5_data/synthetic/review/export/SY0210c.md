## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting for the voice-controlled kitchen timer and noted that the user interface designer was absent at a supplier training day. She relayed the user interface designer's smart speaker comparison, and the team agreed to accept 'set a timer' as well as 'set timer'. The marketing expert presented kitchen gadget trends (matte finishes, white and light grey with one bright accent colour, natural materials) and repeated that the survey showed the timer must be easy to wipe clean. The industrial designer gave firmer prices: 6.00 euros for the electronics, about 1.50 euros for a round matte plastic case and 0.20 euros for the stop button. The team rejected the magnetic back and the rotary dial, added three LEDs to show which timers are running, and chose a matte white body with an orange stop button, for an estimated cost of 8.15 euros. The task of checking whether the speech chip handles accents was first given to the industrial designer, then moved to the project manager, and a top panel task was given to the absent user interface designer.

**actions**
- The user interface designer (absent) will work out the top panel layout, including the positions of the stop button and the three LEDs and how the lights behave, and will assess whether a countdown screen is worth adding.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer her task and the meeting notes.
- The industrial designer will build a clay model of the round case for the detailed design meeting.
- The project manager will ring the speech chip supplier to check whether the chip handles different accents. This task was first given to the industrial designer and was moved to her.
- The marketing expert will prepare criteria for evaluating the final design, based on the survey and the trends research.
- The project manager will send round the minutes of this meeting.

**decisions**
- The timer will accept both 'set timer' and 'set a timer' phrasings.
- There will be no magnetic back. The timer will stand on the counter on rubber feet.
- There will be no rotary dial. The single stop button remains the only physical control.
- Three LEDs, one per timer, will show which timers are running.
- The timer will have a matte white, wipe-clean body with a bright orange stop button.

**problems**
- It is still unknown whether the offline speech chip copes with different accents. The project manager is now checking with the supplier.
- Whether to add a countdown screen (about 2 euros) is undecided and has been passed to the user interface designer.
- The marketing expert's bamboo-effect base was not adopted. It costs about 30 cents more per unit and real bamboo is hard to wipe clean.
- The industrial designer still needs firmer quotes for the case.
