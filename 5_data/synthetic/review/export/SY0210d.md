## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting for the voice-controlled kitchen timer with all four team members present, and the user interface designer confirmed she had received the email with her top panel task. The project manager reported that the speech chip supplier offers a general acoustic model trained on many accents at no extra cost, which the team will use, although strong accents may need slower speech; the industrial designer said someone should test it with real speakers, but nobody took this on. The industrial designer presented the clay model of the round case, about nine centimetres across and four high, with the orange stop button on top, two microphone holes, a rear speaker grille and rubber feet, and gave a firm case quote of 1.60 euros. The user interface designer presented the top panel layout and LED behaviour and recommended against a countdown screen, which the team accepted. The marketing expert presented six evaluation criteria, and the design scored an average of about 1.67 on a one-to-seven scale (one is best), with a final production cost of 8.25 euros against the 12.50 euro limit.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will test the speaker against an extractor fan in the test kitchen next week to confirm the alarm is loud enough.
- The user interface designer will write the quick-start card listing the voice commands, to go in the box. The marketing expert first offered to do this, but she took it because she already has the command phrasings from her smart speaker comparison.
- The project manager will write the final report for management, including the costing and the evaluation.
- The project manager will send round the minutes of this meeting.
- The industrial designer said someone should test the speech chip with people with different accents before sign-off, but nobody took this task.

**decisions**
- The speech chip will use the supplier's general multi-accent acoustic model, at no extra cost.
- The round case is agreed as shown on the clay model: about nine centimetres across and four high, with the stop button in the centre of the top, two microphone holes on the top edge, a speaker grille at the back, the cable out of the back and four rubber feet.
- The three LEDs sit in an arc above the stop button, with raised moulded numbers one to three beside them.
- The LEDs will be white. They stay steady while a timer is running, blink slowly in the last minute and flash fast when the timer finishes, until the user says stop or presses the button.
- No countdown screen or other display will be added.
- A quick-start card with the voice commands will go in the box rather than being printed on the base.
- The final production cost is 8.25 euros per unit: 6.00 for electronics, 1.60 for the case, 0.20 for the stop button and 0.45 for the LEDs and feet.
- The design scored 2 for wipe-clean, 3 for alarm loudness, 1 for messy hands, 2 for looking current, 1 for multiple timers and 1 for price, an average of about 1.67.

**problems**
- The speech chip may struggle with very strong accents, and nobody has taken on testing it with real speakers.
- The alarm has not yet been heard over an extractor fan, so its score of 3 is provisional until the industrial designer's test.
- The marketing expert's small two-digit minutes display (about 90 cents) was set aside because it would need a second button to switch between timers. He still sees a visible number as a selling point.
