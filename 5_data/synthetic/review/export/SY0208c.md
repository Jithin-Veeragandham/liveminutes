## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting and noted that the user interface designer was absent on a training course but had sent his screen sketches. She recapped the functional design decisions and raised the catalogue integration question, which had no owner. The industrial designer reported that RFID tags are deactivated by the pad itself, but barcode items with magnetic strips need a desensitiser plate costing about 250 euros, which the team agreed to include. He presented a freestanding pedestal with a tilted landscape screen. After the marketing expert presented accessibility guidelines on reach range and knee space, the team chose a lower, fixed screen height with knee space over a motorised lift. The revised cost came to about 3,350 euros, 150 euros under the target. The team also agreed on a neutral body with a swappable front panel in each library's colours, and asked for the absent user interface designer's sketches to be redone for landscape with a count-check screen for missed items.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will check which catalogue systems the libraries run and whether SIP2 covers all of them. This was first given to the marketing expert and moved to him because it is tied to the reader and PC.
- The user interface designer (absent) will redo his screen sketches for the landscape screen and add a count-check screen for missed items.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer about his task.
- The project manager will send the minutes out tonight.
- The marketing expert will check with a couple of libraries whether they would use the swappable front panel, and in which colours.
- The industrial designer will make a rough foam mock-up of the casing for the next meeting.

**decisions**
- A desensitiser plate for magnetic-strip barcode items will be included.
- The kiosk will be a freestanding pedestal with a tilted landscape screen at a fixed, lower height of about 90 centimetres, with knee space underneath, instead of a motorised height-adjustable screen.
- The casing will be a neutral grey powder-coated body with a swappable front panel in the library's own colours.
- The interface will include a count-check screen so borrowers can confirm the number of items detected.

**problems**
- The cost margin is tight: the estimate is about 3,350 euros, leaving only about 150 euros under the 3,500 euro target.
- It is not yet confirmed that SIP2 works with all the libraries' catalogue systems.
- The user interface designer's screen sketches are in portrait and must be reworked for the landscape screen.
- Missed items in stacks remain a known risk, which the count-check screen is meant to address.
