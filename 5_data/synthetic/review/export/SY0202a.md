## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, the team introduced themselves, and he presented the project: a self-checkout kiosk for public library networks.
- He gave the budget targets from the brief: a production cost under 4,000 euros per unit and a selling price of around 6,500 euros. The brief also asks that the kiosk fit the look of a library rather than resemble a cash machine.
- The team agreed that the kiosk will handle checkout and renewals but not returns. They also agreed that borrowers will identify themselves with either a library card or a library-app digital card, both read by the same scanner.
- The team discussed barcode versus RFID book reading, an email-only receipt, and a touchscreen-only interface, but did not settle any of these.
- The marketing expert raised accessibility as a key requirement and took on researching it. The project manager then assigned individual preparation tasks for the functional design meeting.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will work out the working design: the internal components and a rough layout.
- The user interface designer will prepare the technical functions design: the step-by-step checkout and renewal flow and the screens.
- The marketing expert will prepare the user requirements, drawing on her meeting with library services staff.
- The marketing expert will gather the accessibility requirements from library services and share them with the user interface designer. The user interface designer had first offered to do this.
- The project manager will send round the project brief and the notes from the meeting.
- Someone should check the cost of RFID readers, but no one took this task.

**decisions**
- The kiosk will support checkout and renewals only; returns stay with the existing book drop.
- Borrowers will identify themselves with a library card or a library-app digital card (QR code), both read by one scanner.
- The production cost target stays fixed at under 4,000 euros per unit.

**problems**
- It is unclear whether the kiosk should read barcodes, RFID tags or both. The cost of RFID readers is unknown, and no one is assigned to check it.
- The team did not decide whether to drop the printed receipt in favour of email or to make printing optional.
- The team did not decide whether the interface should be touchscreen-only or include at least one hardware button such as cancel or help.
