## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, and the team members introduced themselves in their roles as project manager, industrial designer, marketing expert and user interface designer.
- The project manager introduced the project, a hands-free voice-controlled kitchen timer with a target selling price of around 20 euros and a production cost under 10 euros per unit, and explained the four-meeting structure.
- The team discussed initial requirements: an alarm loud enough to hear over an extractor fan, a microphone that can pick up the user over kitchen noise, and voice commands to set, cancel and query the timer.
- The industrial designer said voice recognition is feasible within budget if a cheap offline chip recognising a small fixed set of commands is used, and the team agreed the timer should be voice-first with a physical backup button.
- The team practised with the whiteboard drawing tool, assigned individual tasks, and scheduled the functional design meeting for Friday at 10:00, revised from an earlier plan of Thursday at 14:00 because the industrial designer has a supplier visit on Thursday afternoon.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will research components (voice recognition chip, microphone, speaker, battery) and their approximate costs, including the cheapest adequate recognition module.
- The industrial designer will investigate battery versus mains power, given the drain of an always-listening microphone.
- The user interface designer will draft a voice command list and define what the timer displays or says back.
- The marketing expert will research user requirements for kitchen timers and review competing products on the market.
- The marketing expert will run a quick survey of around ten people who cook a lot, with early responses by the next meeting.
- The project manager will write up and circulate the minutes along with a new invitation for the Friday 10:00 meeting.

**decisions**
- The next meeting (functional design) will be held on Friday at 10:00; this replaces the earlier agreed Thursday 14:00 slot.
- The timer will be primarily voice-controlled but will include at least one physical backup button.
- Voice recognition will be kept simple, using a low-cost offline chip that recognises a small fixed set of commands.

**problems**
- Whether the timer should support multiple simultaneous timers was parked for the functional design meeting, as it complicates the command set.
- Whether a wake word is needed was raised but not settled.
- Battery versus mains power is undecided and affects the display design.
- Meeting the under-10-euro production cost with voice recognition, a loud alarm and a noise-robust microphone remains to be confirmed.
