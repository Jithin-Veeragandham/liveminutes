## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting and explained that the user interface designer was absent all week on an accessibility training course. He confirmed that the minutes and invite had been sent, and the marketing expert confirmed that the survey results and competitor notes were in the shared folder. The industrial designer reported that a dock normally reports a return within ten seconds, but this can take up to about two minutes when mobile signal is poor, notably at the two docks in the underground station car park. To handle this, the team designed a two-stage return confirmation: the phone shows 'bike locked' at once via the dock's Bluetooth lock status, the cost follows when the system confirms, a receipt notification is sent if confirmation is slow, and billing stops at the lock time. On look and feel, the team chose the council's teal as the brand colour, with green and red reserved for status, and a night mode that follows the phone's dark mode setting with an in-app toggle. The marketing expert took over the outdoor readability check from the absent user interface designer, and the next meeting was set for Monday at eleven.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will confirm with the dock supplier that the dock sends its lock status to the phone over Bluetooth.
- The industrial designer will ask the supplier whether the mobile signal at the underground car park docks can be boosted.
- The user interface designer (absent) will add the 'bike locked, confirming' state and the receipt notification to the wireframes.
- The marketing expert will do the outdoor readability check (contrast, text size), taking it over from the user interface designer.
- The marketing expert will chase the council for the final brand guidelines (colours and logo).
- The project manager will send out the minutes, brief the user interface designer on the decisions and ask for her view on the teal shades.
- The project manager will send an invite for the next meeting on Monday at eleven.

**decisions**
- The return confirmation has two stages: 'bike locked' is shown immediately based on the dock's Bluetooth lock status, and the cost is shown once the system confirms the return.
- If the system has not confirmed after about thirty seconds, the app tells the rider a receipt will follow and sends a notification when it arrives.
- Rides are billed up to the moment the bike locks, not when the dock's message reaches the system.
- Teal from the council's brand guidelines is the app's brand colour for buttons and headers, and green and red are used only for status. The exact shades are pending the user interface designer's review.
- The high-contrast night mode follows the phone's own dark mode setting, with a manual toggle in the app.
- No sponsor logo will appear on the confirmation screen for the pilot.
- The outdoor readability check is reassigned from the user interface designer to the marketing expert.
- The next meeting (detailed design) will be on Monday at eleven. This replaces the project manager's initial suggestion of Friday at two.

**problems**
- It is not yet confirmed that the dock sends its lock status to the phone over Bluetooth. The two-stage return confirmation depends on this.
- The mobile signal at the two underground car park docks is poor, so returns there may take up to about two minutes to register.
- The accent colour for the night mode main button (yellow or bright teal on black) is unresolved until the readability check is done.
- The council's brand guidelines are only a draft, and the exact teal shades still need the user interface designer's contrast review.
