## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, noted that the user interface designer was absent, and had the industrial designer and marketing expert introduce themselves.
- He presented the project, a voice-controlled kitchen timer, and explained the series of four meetings, after which the team practised with the whiteboard.
- He gave the financial targets: a selling price of 25 euros, a maximum production cost of 12.50 euros per unit including the voice components, and a profit aim of 50 million euros for the international market.
- The team raised concerns that voice recognition chips could strain the budget and that an international market implies multiple languages.
- In the initial brainstorm the team agreed on multiple named timers and voice-first control with a physical stop button as backup; a phone app was suggested but set aside, and battery versus mains power was left open.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will investigate the working design: components, power source, microphone and speaker.
- The marketing expert will research user requirements through a small survey and a review of existing kitchen timers.
- The user interface designer, though absent, was assigned to design the voice commands and the timer's spoken responses.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer the brief and her assignment.
- Checking the cost of voice recognition chips was mentioned but nobody took it on.

**decisions**
- The timer will support multiple named timers.
- The timer will be voice-first, with a physical stop button as backup.

**problems**
- The cost of voice recognition chips is unknown and may be hard to fit within the 12.50 euro production cost; no one is responsible for checking it.
- Selling internationally means the voice control may need to support several languages.
- Battery versus mains power is undecided; the industrial designer leans towards battery.
- The idea of a phone app was parked and not resolved.
