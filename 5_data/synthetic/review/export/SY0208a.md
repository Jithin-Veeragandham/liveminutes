## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, noted that the industrial designer was absent through illness, and had the marketing expert and user interface designer introduce themselves.
- She presented the project: a self-checkout kiosk sold to public library authorities, with a production cost target of under 3,500 euros per unit including screen, scanner, receipt printer and casing.
- The team agreed that a single kiosk should handle both borrowing and returns, and discussed whether the kiosk should read RFID tags, barcodes or both, which was handed to the absent industrial designer along with the question of whether a receipt printer is needed.
- The marketing expert described a very broad user group, especially older borrowers, children and non-native speakers, which led to calls for a simple interface with big buttons and language selection by language name.
- A card-free face recognition idea from the user interface designer was parked over privacy concerns, and the task of obtaining library usage data moved from the marketing expert to the user interface designer.

**actions**
- The industrial designer (absent) will compare RFID pads and barcode scanners and check whether supporting both fits the budget.
- The industrial designer (absent) will check whether a receipt printer is needed.
- The user interface designer will obtain checkout usage numbers and peak times from the library; this task was originally given to the marketing expert.
- The marketing expert will research existing self-checkout kiosks on the market, their costs and common complaints.
- The user interface designer will draft a rough user flow (card, books, confirm, receipt).
- The project manager will email the industrial designer about his tasks and send the minutes to everyone.

**decisions**
- The kiosk will handle both borrowing and returns on one unit.
- Language selection will use language names rather than flags.
- The whiteboard tool exercise was skipped because a team member was absent.

**problems**
- It is unresolved whether the kiosk must support both RFID and barcodes and whether that fits the 3,500 euro cost target.
- It is unclear whether a receipt printer is needed.
- Face recognition sign-in was parked due to library privacy concerns.
- The user base is very broad, so the interface must work for older borrowers, children and non-native speakers.
