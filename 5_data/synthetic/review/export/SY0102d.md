## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting and explained that the user interface designer was absent because he was ill with the flu. She reported that the minutes of the conceptual design meeting were in the shared folder and that the client had approved the concept on the condition that the cup matches its exact dark green.
- The industrial designer showed the matte sample with the slider sip lid from the Rotterdam plant, which the team found much less cheap-looking but slightly too blue and light. The team chose a custom colour masterbatch at about four cents extra per cup, bringing the cost breakdown to about two euros eleven per cup, well under the three-euro limit.
- The marketing expert reported that eight students at a campus shop liked the matte finish, the stacking and the return line, though three found the slider stiff; the team kept the slider lid and stayed with one size despite a request for a bigger cup.
- The team discussed the absent user interface designer's counter sign artwork, considered that he might find the wording too long, and kept 'any of our forty shops' on the sign. The marketing expert's evaluation scored the design twenty-six out of thirty, and the project manager declared the final design agreed.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will ask the Rotterdam plant for a second sample with the custom colour masterbatch matched to the client's green.
- The user interface designer will send the print-ready vector files for the cup markings and counter sign to the plant when he is back, using the new green codes; this was first given to the industrial designer and then moved to the user interface designer because only he has the vector files.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer today with the meeting outcomes, the counter sign wording decision and the request to send the print files.
- The marketing expert will write up the evaluation and the student feedback as a page for the final report.
- The project manager will write the final report and send it to the client once the second sample is available.

**decisions**
- The cup will use a custom colour masterbatch to match the client's exact dark green, at about four cents extra, bringing the cost to about two euros eleven per cup.
- The slider sip lid is kept despite some students finding it stiff.
- The cup stays in one size; the industrial designer will not get a quote for a second mould.
- The counter sign will say 'any of our forty shops', while the cup keeps the line about returning it to any counter.
- The final design is agreed: a dark green matte, slightly tapered polypropylene cup with a white logo, a slider sip lid and the matched colour, in one size.

**problems**
- The colour match must still be confirmed with a second sample, which takes about two weeks; the client's approval depends on the exact green.
- The slider on the lid is stiff when new, which three of eight students found fiddly; it reportedly loosens after a few washes.
- The user interface designer has not yet seen the decision to keep 'any of our forty shops' on the counter sign, and it is unknown whether it fits his layout.
- The print-ready vector files are only on the user interface designer's laptop, so sending artwork to the plant waits for his return.
- Some students asked for a bigger cup; a second size remains parked and may be mentioned to the client as a later option.
