## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting and noted that the user interface designer was absent on a first aid training course but had sent his final layout. She confirmed that she had emailed the industrial designer about his electrician and mounting task and had sent round the minutes.
- The industrial designer reported an electrician's quote of three hundred and twenty pounds for a new mains supply at the underground library car park, and the project manager reported that the estates office will pay for it from its building maintenance budget.
- The team chose navy steel posts for the four surface car parks and a wall bracket for the library car park, signed off the user interface designer's layout, and assigned the absent user interface designer to produce five print-ready name strip files for the supplier.
- The project manager costed the signs at three hundred and ninety pounds for each surface sign and three hundred and seventy-two pounds for the library sign, one thousand nine hundred and thirty-two pounds in total, and the team agreed to keep the nightly counter reset and decide on a midday reset after hand spot counts.
- The marketing expert evaluated the design against the survey findings, and the team judged that it meets the brief. The team agreed there would be no QR code in this phase, and the app and the central sign remain later ideas.

**actions**
- The user interface designer (absent) is to produce five print-ready vector files for the car park name strips, to the supplier's specification, by the end of next week. The project manager assigned this.
- The project manager will email the user interface designer about the print files tonight, copying in the industrial designer with the specification.
- The industrial designer will forward the supplier's template to the project manager.
- The industrial designer will send the final specification (casing, posts, bracket and LED panels) to the supplier on Monday.
- The industrial designer will book the electrician for the library car park supply with the estates office before term.
- The marketing expert will do hand spot counts on a couple of afternoons in the first fortnight after installation, to check counter drift.
- The marketing expert will draft a piece for the campus newsletter explaining what the colours mean.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and the final design report and send them to the estates office.

**decisions**
- The estates office will pay for the new library car park mains supply from its building maintenance budget, outside the sign budget.
- The four surface car park signs will be mounted on navy-painted galvanised steel posts about two point two metres high, and the library car park sign on a wall bracket.
- The user interface designer's final layout is signed off: a navy strip with the car park name in white, a P symbol on the left, the LED number on a green, amber or red panel, and no flashing.
- The nightly counter reset with attendant correction is kept. Whether a midday reset is needed will be decided after the spot counts.
- There will be no QR code on the casing in this phase.
- The app and the central sign near the main gate remain later ideas.

**problems**
- The budget margin is only ten pounds on each surface sign, at three hundred and ninety pounds per sign.
- Counter drift from tailgating could reach four or five cars by the afternoon on busy days, which is significant when red is at five free spaces. A midday reset is undecided pending the spot counts.
- The marketing expert's statement that a full car park would show the word FULL instead of zero was not agreed. The industrial designer noted that the LED panel shows only digits, and the point was left unresolved.
- Ordering the panels depends on print files from the user interface designer, who was absent.
- The app many users asked for, and the central sign, are not part of this phase.
