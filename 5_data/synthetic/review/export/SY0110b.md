## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, welcomed back the marketing expert, who had been at the Cologne trade fair during the kickoff, and recapped the requirements from the kickoff: the cup must be dishwasher-safe and fit standard takeaway lids, with a one-euro deposit, a production cost under three euros and fifty partner cafes in the first year.
- On the cup itself, the industrial designer compared stainless steel (about 3.80 euros, over budget), bamboo fibre (melamine-bound and unsuitable for hot dishwashers), collapsible silicone (about 2.60 euros, but hard to clean and the flexing rim does not hold a lid) and polypropylene (about 1.20 euros and dishwasher-proof). The team chose polypropylene and dropped the silicone idea. Based on the cafe owners' feedback reported by the marketing expert, the cup will be stackable with a tapered shape, have a 90 mm rim to fit the common lid size, and have space for a cafe logo.
- On returns, the user interface designer compared supermarket bottle machines with existing cup schemes and recommended a counter return at any partner cafe, where staff refund the euro. The tracking app stays shelved, which matches the cafe owners' wish for no extra steps at the counter. Return machines and a two-euro deposit were both suggested and rejected. To show customers where they can return cups, the team liked the idea of a partner badge in cafe windows that matches the logo on the cup.
- The team discussed lost cups again: with polypropylene, a cafe loses only about twenty cents per unreturned cup. For the dishwasher durability test, the team first agreed on the office dishwasher but then revised the plan. The final plan is to run the samples for a week in the commercial dishwasher of Café Lindner, a cafe owner the marketing expert met at the fair.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will prepare polypropylene samples by Monday and run the dishwasher durability test at Café Lindner for a week.
- The marketing expert will email the owner of Café Lindner this week to arrange the test.
- The marketing expert will write up her notes from the trade fair and send them round.
- The user interface designer will sketch the counter return flow and the partner badge for the next meeting.
- The project manager will write the minutes.

**decisions**
- The cup will be made of polypropylene; the collapsible silicone idea is dropped.
- The cup will have a 90 mm rim to fit standard lids, will be stackable, and will have space for a cafe logo.
- Cups will be returned at the counter of any partner cafe, where staff refund the deposit; there will be no app and no return machines.
- The deposit stays at one euro.
- Partner cafes will be marked with a window badge that matches the logo on the cup.
- The dishwasher test will run for a week at Café Lindner (revised from the original plan to test in the office dishwasher on Thursday).

**problems**
- Cafes still lose about twenty cents for every cup that is not returned; who absorbs this has not been settled.
- Polypropylene must still prove its durability in the commercial dishwasher test.
- The test depends on the owner of Café Lindner confirming the arrangement.
