## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting, welcomed back the industrial designer after his illness, and confirmed that the decisions email and the functional design minutes had been sent.
- The industrial designer presented his component research. A single cheap microphone misses about one in three wake words over fan noise, while a two-microphone set with a noise filter chip (about 2.80 euros) copes with a fan but only within about two metres of the cook. He also confirmed that a loud enough speaker (about 1.20 euros), the stop button and the three coloured LEDs are all affordable.
- Because of the microphone's limited range, the team revised its earlier choice of a fridge magnet and decided on a small countertop stand placed near the hob.
- The user interface designer presented the voice command list (wake word, colour, time, with the first free timer used if no colour is given) and a display sketch with three countdown rows beside the coloured lights. The front will be tilted back so it can be read from above.
- The marketing expert presented kitchen trends (matte finishes, sage green, off-white, wood looks), and the team chose a rounded matte off-white body with a wood-effect top, powered by two AA batteries. The rough cost is about 9.50 to 10 euros, within the 12.50 euro limit.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will produce a full cost breakdown for the detailed design meeting.
- The industrial designer will build a model of the countertop stand.
- The user interface designer will finish the display design for the tilted front and finalise the voice command list.
- The marketing expert will propose a wake word.
- The marketing expert will prepare the criteria for evaluating the design.
- The project manager will write up and distribute the minutes.

**decisions**
- The timer will use a two-microphone set with a noise filter chip.
- The timer will be a small freestanding unit on the counter near the hob, not a fridge magnet. This revised an earlier decision in the same meeting.
- The physical stop button is confirmed, together with a speaker loud enough to be heard over a fan and three coloured LEDs.
- The casing will be rounded and easy to wipe, with a matte off-white body and a wood-effect plastic top.
- The front of the timer will be tilted back so the display can be read from above.
- The timer will be powered by two AA batteries rather than a rechargeable battery.

**problems**
- The wake word is still undecided. 'Hey Chef' was suggested but considered cheesy.
- The microphone only works reliably within about two metres of the cook, so the timer has to be placed near the hob.
- The marketing expert regretted losing the benefit of keeping the timer off the crowded counter.
- The cost figure of about 9.50 to 10 euros is only a rough estimate until the full breakdown is done.
