## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting and the team introduced themselves. The project is a new app for CityCycle, the city bike-share operator, that will work with its roughly 120 docking stations. CityCycle wants the app to replace its current one, which requires users to type a six-digit dock code. The app has an 80,000 euro budget, must be free to download, and should launch next spring.
- On target users, the marketing expert reported that about seventy percent of trips are weekday commuter trips, with many first-time tourist riders at weekends. The team agreed that commuters are the primary target group, who mainly need speed, and that tourists are a secondary group to support. How tourists without an account would rent a bike was left open. The user interface designer suggested a web page reached from a QR code with mobile payment.
- On how the app connects to the dock, the industrial designer first proposed a kiosk screen on each dock. The team set this aside because the budget covers only the app and any dock changes need a separate budget that may not exist. The team agreed on a phone-first design in which each dock only carries a cheap identifier, such as an NFC tag or a QR sticker.
- For user research, the marketing expert will run an online survey through CityCycle's mailing list and analyse CityCycle's trip data. This replaces the street interviews at three docks that the team had agreed on earlier in the meeting.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will send an online survey through CityCycle's mailing list and analyse the trip data, with early results by the Thursday meeting.
- The user interface designer will send a few survey questions about the current app to the marketing expert by tomorrow.
- The user interface designer will map the user flow from opening the app to unlocking a bike and review other bike-share apps.
- The industrial designer will compare NFC, QR codes and possibly Bluetooth for identifying docks, looking at cost and reliability.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.
- The project manager will ask CityCycle about a dock hardware budget and whether it would want dock screens later.

**decisions**
- Commuters are the primary target users. Tourists are a secondary group that the app should support.
- The app will be phone-first. No kiosk screens will be designed for now, and each dock only needs a cheap identifier such as an NFC tag or a QR sticker.
- User research will be an online survey through CityCycle's mailing list, plus analysis of CityCycle's trip data. This replaces the street interviews agreed earlier in the meeting.
- The next meeting is the functional design meeting, on Thursday morning.

**problems**
- It is not settled how tourists without an account would rent a bike. A QR-linked web page with mobile payment was suggested, but it would be extra work to build.
- It is unknown whether CityCycle has any separate budget for changes to the docks.
- The dock identification technology (NFC, QR or Bluetooth) has not been chosen. QR codes may be unreliable in the dark or with slow cameras.
