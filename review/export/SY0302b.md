## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, welcomed the marketing expert back after his absence from the kick-off, and recapped the earlier decisions and open questions.
- The industrial designer presented component research: offline fixed-vocabulary voice chips cost about 3.80 Euro, online recognition is far over budget, and kitchen noise above about 65 decibels may cause missed commands, so he recommended two microphones.
- The user interface designer presented a small command set with named timers, short spoken confirmations and a three-row display. The marketing expert's research supported named timers and showed most users run two or three timers at once.
- The marketing expert reported that most people time cooking with their phone or oven timer, and that users like a wake word because it prevents false triggers, although 'Hey Chef' got a mixed reaction.
- The industrial designer declared that the timer would be fully waterproof, but nobody agreed and the question was left open. A task for the user interface designer to record kitchen noise clips was created and then cancelled because the chip maker supplies a noise test kit.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will prepare a cost breakdown (chip, two microphones, display) and check whether a larger-vocabulary chip is needed.
- The user interface designer will turn the command list into a full interaction flow covering three timers and the display.
- The marketing expert will test two or three two-syllable wake word names with the people from his research.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and send them round.

**decisions**
- Voice recognition will run offline on the device with a fixed vocabulary.
- Timers can be given names (such as 'pasta'), rather than being numbered.
- The timer will support a maximum of three simultaneous timers.
- The physical stop button stops all timers that are currently ringing.
- The timer will have a wake word. The name is still to be decided.

**problems**
- The fixed vocabulary of about fifty words may be too small for numbers, commands and timer names, so a larger chip may be needed.
- Kitchen noise such as an extractor fan can exceed the chip's roughly 65 decibel limit. A second microphone is recommended but must fit the budget.
- Whether the timer should be waterproof or splash-proof is still unresolved.
- The wake word name is not yet chosen. 'Hey Chef' received mixed reactions.
