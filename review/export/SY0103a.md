## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kick-off meeting, the team members introduced themselves and their roles, and the project manager introduced the project: a voice-controlled kitchen timer that people can use when their hands are dirty from cooking.
- The project manager presented the financial targets: a selling price of 25 euros, a maximum production cost of 12.50 euros per unit and an overall profit target of 15 million euros.
- On the budget, the industrial designer estimated a simple command-recognition chip at three to four euros and raised the open question of battery versus mains power, since always listening for a wake word drains batteries and a mains adapter adds cost. The marketing expert's idea of a recipe screen with wifi was judged not to fit the budget.
- On voice interaction, the team agreed that speech recognition should run on the device with a small fixed set of commands rather than through a phone app. It should use a wake word, probably a distinctive product name. It should give short spoken confirmations, and one physical button should serve as a backup for stopping the alarm.
- Support for multiple named timers was raised as a mainly software feature to explore, and individual research tasks were assigned before the functional design meeting.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will research the components (speech chip, microphone, speaker) and the power question, and produce a rough cost breakdown against the 12.50 euro limit.
- The user interface designer will work out the voice interaction: wake word, command list, spoken feedback and multiple timers.
- The marketing expert will research what users want from kitchen timers, what they complain about, and whether customers will pay 25 euros for a timer.
- The marketing expert will come up with candidate names for the wake word.
- The project manager will write up the minutes and send them to the team.

**decisions**
- The target selling price is 25 euros and production cost must not exceed 12.50 euros per unit.
- Speech recognition will run on the device itself with a small fixed command set (e.g. set, stop, how long left), not via a phone app.
- The timer will use a wake word, probably a distinctive name rather than a common word like 'timer'.
- The timer will have one physical button as a backup, mainly for stopping the alarm.
- The timer will give short spoken confirmations of commands.
- A recipe screen with wifi is out of scope because it does not fit the budget.

**problems**
- Whether the timer will run on batteries or mains power is still open, because always-on listening drains batteries while a mains adapter adds cost and a cable.
- It is not yet known whether all components fit within 12.50 euros.
- The marketing expert questioned whether customers will pay 25 euros when ordinary kitchen timers cost around five euros.
- Support for multiple simultaneous timers was raised but not yet decided.
