## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the conceptual design meeting, welcomed back the industrial designer, and reported that CityCycle had confirmed in writing that stickers or tags on the docks count as maintenance and will be fitted by its maintenance crew on normal rounds.
- The marketing expert reported that about twelve percent of survey respondents have phones that cannot read NFC tags, and that commuters (about 800 of 1,040 respondents) care mainly about unlock speed, while weekend riders care about finding bikes and knowing the price, although only about sixty respondents were tourists.
- The industrial designer presented his comparison of NFC, QR and Bluetooth. Bluetooth was ruled out because it needs powered beacons in the docks, and he recommended a combined sticker with an NFC tag inside and a QR code on the front, at about 1.50 euros each and under 250 euros for all docks plus spares.
- The team agreed on the combined sticker, with NFC as the main method and QR as the fallback, and on tamper-evident stickers plus an app that only accepts CityCycle QR codes. The industrial designer's GPS-based unlock idea was rejected.
- The user interface designer presented wireframes of the five core screens, and the team adopted them as the concept, with numbers added to the map pins and the single-ride price shown to guests before they pay.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will order samples of combined NFC and QR stickers from two suppliers and test them outdoors, in the dark and in the rain.
- The project manager will ask CityCycle when its maintenance crew can fit the stickers. This was first suggested for the marketing expert and taken over by the project manager because he has the CityCycle contact.
- The user interface designer will turn the wireframes into a clickable prototype, including the tap and scan screens, numbers on the map pins and the error for fake QR codes, for the detailed design meeting.
- The marketing expert will draft the guest mode text explaining what a single ride costs.
- The marketing expert will recruit five commuters from the survey volunteers to try the prototype.
- The project manager will send round the minutes today.

**decisions**
- Each dock will carry a combined sticker with an NFC tag inside and a QR code printed on the front. NFC is the main unlock method and QR is the fallback for older phones and anyone who cannot tap.
- Bluetooth will not be used, because it needs powered beacons in the docks and there is no dock hardware budget.
- Stickers will be tamper-evident, and the app will only accept CityCycle QR codes, showing an error for any other code.
- The wireframes are adopted as the concept: the app opens on the map with one large unlock button, the return confirmation screen shows ride time and price and has a report fault button, and guest mode is a ride-without-account option on the welcome screen with Apple Pay or Google Pay.
- Map pins will show numbers of bikes and free docks, not only colours, so they work for colour-blind users.
- The guest screen will show the single-ride price before payment.
- The next meeting is the detailed design meeting on Friday morning, moved from Wednesday so the sticker samples can be tested first.

**problems**
- Data on tourists is still thin, because only about sixty survey respondents were tourists.
- The durability and readability of the combined stickers outdoors and in the dark have not yet been tested.
- The dates when CityCycle's crew can fit the stickers are not yet known.
- On some iPhones the app may need to be open before an NFC tag can be read.
- Single-ride prices and wording for the guest screen are not yet available.
