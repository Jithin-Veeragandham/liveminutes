## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the kickoff meeting, introduced the team and presented the project: a voice-controlled kitchen timer that sits on the counter and can be operated hands-free.
- She presented the budget: a selling price of 25 euros, a maximum production cost of 12.50 euros per unit, and a profit target of 50 million euros.
- The team discussed the target group and first features. The final target group is home cooks. This replaced an earlier choice of professional kitchens, which the team revised because of the price point and the kitchen noise.
- The user interface designer raised the wake word problem, the alarm, and a simple display. The industrial designer pointed out that a custom wake word and a spoken alarm would increase the hardware cost.
- The project manager assigned research tasks to each team member. The next meeting, the functional design meeting, is in thirty minutes.

**actions**
- The marketing expert will research what home cooks need from a kitchen timer, possibly with a quick survey.
- The industrial designer will investigate voice recognition chips and whether they fit within the 12.50 euro production cost. He will also look at battery versus mains power.
- The user interface designer will work out the wake word, the voice commands (set, cancel, time remaining), the display and a button fallback.
- The project manager will send round the minutes.

**decisions**
- The target group is home cooks in ordinary households. This was revised during the meeting from an earlier choice of professional kitchens.
- The timer will support multiple named timers.
- The display will use large, readable digits rather than a colour screen.
- The timer will have a physical button fallback in case voice commands are not recognised.
- The team will meet again in thirty minutes for the functional design meeting.

**problems**
- It is unknown whether a voice recognition chip fits within the 12.50 euro production cost.
- The power source (battery or mains) is undecided, because always-on listening consumes power.
- It is undecided whether the wake word will be fixed or user-selectable. A user-selectable wake word needs a more expensive chip.
- It is undecided whether the alarm will announce which timer finished or only beep. A spoken alarm needs a speaker and adds cost.
- The device should be easy to clean and at least splash-proof, but this has not been specified further.
