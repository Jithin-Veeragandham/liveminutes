## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting for the voice-controlled kitchen timer and welcomed back the user interface designer, who had missed the kickoff and confirmed she had received the email with her tasks.
- The marketing expert presented findings from last year's kitchen appliance survey: most people time cooking with the oven or their phone, messy hands make phones awkward, cooks often time two or three things at once, alarms must be loud enough to hear over an extractor fan, and people dislike changing batteries even more than they dislike cables.
- The industrial designer presented component options and rough costs: a two-microphone noise-cancelling setup at about 1.80 euros, an offline fixed-vocabulary speech chip at about 2.50 euros, a plug-in adapter at about 1.20 euros and a speaker at about 0.60 euros.
- The team chose mains power, two microphones and an offline chip. The user interface designer presented a short fixed command set with the wake word 'timer', spoken confirmation and a loud beep alarm, and the team adopted up to three numbered timers.
- The single physical button will be a large stop button on top. Food-named timers and a rotary dial were proposed but set aside, and tasks were assigned for the conceptual design meeting.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will get firmer prices for the two-microphone setup, the speech chip, the plug-in adapter and the speaker.
- The user interface designer will compare the team's voice commands with the timer commands used by smart speakers. The marketing expert first offered to do this, but she took it because she had already started on it.
- The marketing expert will research trends in kitchen gadgets, including colours and materials.
- The project manager will send round the minutes of this meeting.
- The project manager said that someone should check whether the speech chip handles accents, but nobody took this task.

**decisions**
- The timer will be mains powered (plug-in) rather than battery powered.
- The timer will use a two-microphone noise-cancelling setup.
- Speech recognition will use an offline chip with a small fixed vocabulary.
- The command set is 'set timer [time]', 'how long left', 'add a minute', 'stop' and 'cancel', with 'timer' as the provisional wake word. The timer repeats each command back and gives a loud beep and a spoken announcement when a timer finishes.
- Up to three simultaneous timers will be supported, called timer one, two and three.
- The one physical button will be a large stop button on top of the timer.

**problems**
- Nobody knows whether the speech chip copes with different accents, and nobody has taken on checking it.
- Electronics already come to about 6.10 euros before the case, against the 12.50 euro production cost limit.
- A power cable on the counter is still unpopular with users, even though batteries are disliked more.
- The marketing expert's idea of built-in food names for timers was parked because of vocabulary limits.
- The industrial designer's rotary dial for manual setting was deferred to the conceptual design meeting, pending full costs.
- The magnetic back from the kickoff meeting is still to be discussed at the conceptual design meeting.
