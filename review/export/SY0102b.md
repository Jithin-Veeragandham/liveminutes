## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the functional design meeting, welcomed back the industrial designer, and reported on her actions from the kickoff: she had emailed him the slides and action list, and the client had said that unreturned cups are a problem because it wants to present the cups as a sustainability measure; she had not yet put the corporate colours in the shared folder.
- The industrial designer presented his materials research, ruling out stainless steel on cost and melamine-bound bamboo fibre on health and heat grounds, and proposed polypropylene for cup and lid at about one euro eighty per cup including the lid, lasting about five hundred uses and withstanding the cafés' dishwasher temperatures of about sixty degrees wash and eighty-five degrees rinse.
- On the deposit, the marketing expert reported that the largest German scheme charges one euro per cup, that schemes at fifty cents see return rates of about sixty percent and schemes at one euro or more about eighty-five percent, and that returning at any shop helps; the team settled on a deposit of one euro.
- On returns, the user interface designer compared a QR code with an app, a physical token, and using the cup itself as proof of deposit; given the marketing expert's evidence that apps lower uptake and tokens get lost, the team chose to return the cup itself at the counter of any of the forty shops.
- The meeting closed with tasks for the conceptual design meeting, including a sample cup, cup markings, a counter sign, competitor research and a comparison of lid types.

**actions**
- The project manager will put the client's corporate colours in the shared folder today.
- The project manager will check with the client whether it accepts losing about eighty cents on each cup that is not returned at a one-euro deposit.
- The industrial designer will ask the Rotterdam plant for a sample cup and a quote for a matte finish.
- The industrial designer will compare a sip lid and a flip lid, including their costs, for the next meeting.
- The user interface designer will work out the markings on the cup, including logo placement and how users know the cup can be returned.
- The user interface designer will design the counter sign explaining the deposit and return; this was first given to the marketing expert and then moved to the user interface designer.
- The marketing expert will research what competitors' reusable cups look like, including trending colours and shapes.

**decisions**
- The cup and lid will both be made of polypropylene.
- The deposit will be one euro per cup, with the project manager checking with the client about the loss on unreturned cups.
- Customers return the cup itself at the counter of any of the client's shops to get the deposit back; there will be no app and no token.
- The counter sign task was moved from the marketing expert to the user interface designer.

**problems**
- Each unreturned cup costs the client about eighty cents more than the one-euro deposit, and it is not yet known whether the client accepts this.
- Whether to print a QR code on the bottom of the cup so the client can track cups in circulation is left open.
- There is a concern that a polypropylene cup could look cheap; a matte finish is being considered.
- The lid type, sip lid or flip lid, has not been chosen.
