## Gold: summary (F1)

**abstract**
- The project manager opened the detailed design meeting and confirmed that everyone had received the minutes of the conceptual design meeting.
- The industrial designer and the user interface designer presented the mock-up of the counter-top timer with a flat display window, a row of ten timer icons, a rotary dial and a cancel button that was moved further from the dial and made square after it was pressed by mistake during testing.
- The industrial designer presented the cost breakdown: 11.10 euros per unit, or 12.00 euros with the 0.90 euro backup battery. A third sage green colour would add about 0.40 euros per unit in setup fees, so the team revised its earlier agreement on sage green and chose to include the backup battery and keep only white and black.
- The user interface designer presented the final forty-word vocabulary and the alarm wording, three beeps followed by '[name] timer is done', repeated every ten seconds until cancelled.
- The marketing expert evaluated the design against five survey criteria on a scale of one to seven. All scored one or two except alarm loudness over an extractor fan, which scored three because the speaker has not been tested.

**actions**
- The industrial designer will test the speaker next to a running extractor fan.
- The industrial designer will update the cost sheet to include the backup battery and remove the sage green colour.
- The user interface designer will send round the final vocabulary list with example phrases.
- The marketing expert will write up the evaluation.
- The project manager will put together the final report and send round the minutes.

**decisions**
- The cancel button is moved about two centimetres below the dial and made square so it can be told apart from the round dial by touch.
- The backup battery (0.90 euros) is included, bringing the unit cost to 12.00 euros and leaving 0.50 euros of margin.
- The timer will come in white and black only. Sage green was agreed at the start of the meeting but dropped after the cost breakdown showed a setup fee of about 0.40 euros per unit.
- The vocabulary uses exactly forty words: the wake word, the commands, the ten timer names and the number words.
- The alarm beeps three times and then says '[name] timer is done', for example 'pasta timer is done', and repeats every ten seconds until the user says cancel or presses the button. The alarm phrases are stored audio and do not use the chip's vocabulary.
- The design scored one for hands-free use, one for telling timers apart, three for alarm loudness, two for ease of cleaning and one for not changing batteries.

**problems**
- The speaker's loudness over an extractor fan has not been tested, which is why that criterion scored only three.
- The margin is only 0.50 euros per unit, so any component price increase could push the timer over budget.
- Sage green might come back as a later edition if the product sells, but this was not agreed.
- The timer is wipe-clean but not dishwasher safe.
